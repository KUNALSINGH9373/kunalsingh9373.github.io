Third-Party AI Evaluations and Audit Standards: Reading List

How to use this list

This is a curated map of the third-party AI evaluation and auditing field as of late October 2026: the organizations setting standards, the standards themselves, and the most-cited papers, preprints, forum posts, newsletters and news pieces. Each entry has a link and a 1–2 line summary.

  • Scope: independent / third-party evaluation, frontier AI auditing, eval standards and assurance, evaluator access and safe harbor, and the science of evals.

  • Out of scope: pure capability benchmarks and general AI governance unless they bear directly on who checks AI systems and how.

  • Ranking: foundational papers are ordered by how much later work builds on them (a judgment, not live citation counts); forum posts by LessWrong karma when read.

Organizations

Three new organizations now anchor the field: AVERI does audit design and pilot audits, the AI Evaluator Forum sets evaluator-side operating standards, and Guidelight sets developer-side practice standards and grades companies against them.

AVERI — AI Verification and Evaluation Research Institute

averi.org · 501(c)(3) nonprofit launched January 15, 2026 by Miles Brundage (ex-OpenAI policy lead). Mission: "make frontier AI auditing effective and universal." Fortune reported $7.5M raised toward a $13M two-year budget; AVERI takes no money from frontier AI companies or their executives. It began as a think tank and now runs voluntary pilot audits.

AI Evaluator Forum (AEF)

aievaluatorforum.org · A voluntary network of independent third-party evaluators, formed December 2025; not a legal entity. Members: AVERI, Collective Intelligence Project, Meridian Labs, METR, Princeton HAL, RAND, SecureBio and Transluce (members). Chair: Conrad Stosz.

  • AEF-1: Minimum Operating Conditions for Independent Third Party AI Evaluations (v1, Dec 4, 2025). A standard plus checklist evaluators fill in to show how an eval met five principles: sufficient access and resources, minimized conflicts of interest, analytic autonomy, transparent methods and results, and protection of sensitive information (26 conditions, 15 required). Used in METR's May 2026 Frontier Risk Report, Transluce's mental-health eval and SecureBio's GPT-6 Astra assessment. AEF says the EU AI Office endorsed key provisions as a way to meet the GPAI Code of Practice's independence rules. Latent Space (Sep 2026) reported xAI, OpenAI and Anthropic cosigning; AEF's own page does not list company signatories.

  • Minimum Conditions for Embedding Evaluators (Sep 18, 2026; 200+ signatories incl. Hinton, Russell, Steinhardt, Brundage). Open letter setting five conditions for evaluators embedded inside labs: real independence and editorial control, multiple evaluators per risk area, transparency with limited NDAs, anti-retaliation protection, and access equal to privileged internal staff.

  • The Path Ahead. Roadmap: stronger conflict-of-interest provisions, pooled independence-preserving funding, talent pipeline.

Guidelight

guidelight.ai · Independent nonprofit founded by Steven Adler and Page Hedley (both ex-OpenAI safety); first standards published May 19, 2026. Three programs: Standards, Assessments, Incentives. Accepts no funding from AI companies or their staff.

Foundational and most-cited papers

Almost every current proposal traces back to five works: Brundage et al. 2020 (verifiable claims), Raji et al. 2020 and 2022 (internal and outsider audits), Shevlane et al. 2023 (extreme-risk evals) and Casper et al. 2024 (black-box access is not enough).

Suggested reading order and open gaps

Start with three pieces, about a day of reading: AVERI's Frontier AI Auditing, AEF-1, and Casper et al. on black-box access.

  1. Core frame: Frontier AI Auditing → AEF-1 → Guidelight Control + scorecard.

  2. Roots: Brundage et al. 2020 → Outsider Oversight → Black-Box Access is Insufficient → Safe Harbor.

  3. Access and independence now: Embedded Assessments, Who Funds Frontier AI Evaluations?, RUSI secure access, Is METR a Meaningful Check?.

  4. Can evals be trusted?: Sonnet 4.5's eval gaming, Auditing Games for Sandbagging, Measuring What Matters.

Open gaps:

  • No accreditation body or "auditor of auditors" exists yet; AVERI's PCAOB-style proposal is unbuilt.

  • Funding independence is unsolved: most third-party evals are still paid for or enabled by the developer evaluated.

  • Publication rights for embedded evaluators are contested (see Reuters, Oct 2026).

  • Evaluation awareness may make behavioral evals unreliable; white-box access is the main proposed fix.

  • Only one binding audit mandate (Illinois, from 2028) exists, so audit scope and standards there are still to be defined.
